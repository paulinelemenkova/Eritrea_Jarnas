\Abstract{The application of the Artificial Intelligence (AI) in satellite image is essential for mapping environmental dynamics of the Earth's landscapes. A vital source of geo-information in environmental science, remote sensing (RS) data present valuable source of information on Earth's landscapes. Nevertheless, advanced methods are crucial for their processing and analysis. Here, we adopt the AI algorithms in RS technologies driven by the programming algorithms of Scikit-Learn library of Python embedded in the GRASS GIS software. The aim is to improve image classification through the AI-enhanced algorithm workflow. Here we showed that the process of multi-step fitting in image analysis drives the predictive modelling of the landscape dynamics depending on the selection of the algorithm. We employed four AI algorithms using GRASS GIS software with four Machine Learning (ML) algorithms : Gaussian Naive Bayes (GND), Decision Tree Classifier (DTC), Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) and Support Vector Machine (SVM). The results demonstrated that  that SVM has the better capability of pattern recognition with enhanced accuracy and efficiency of image analysis and classification. Time-series satellite Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS data spanning spring seasons of 2014–2024 was retrieved from the USGS platform. The methodology includes training, fitting and prediction. The effectiveness of ML algorithms was tested to evaluate their efficiency in classification of a time series of the satellite images using threshold  and multi-step fitting. The results demonstrated landscape dynamics in the coastal and desert areas of Eritrea. The results show that in 2014 the bare land or sparse vegetation class has the largest percentage (15,253 $km^2$ or 42\% of the total), followed by shrubs (8,024 $km^2$ or 21.7\%), herbaceous vegetation (5,268 $km^2$ or 14.8\%) and agricultural crops (1714 $km^2$ or 4.2\%). In 2024, the largest increase for herbaceous vegetation was detected in the area of Semenawi Keih Bahri (> 726.25 $km^2$), while the largest decline category of bare lands with 760 $km^2$. The increase in land cover types was recorded for the classes urban areas built, cultivated agricultural lands, and forests, while shrubs and herbaceous vegetation declined during recent decade.
