\abstract{Remote sensing (RS) data provide excellent source of information for Earth observation. Such data are widely used in environmental monitoring and mapping landscape dynamics. The advanced methods of processing RS data, such as Machine Learning (ML), are essential for proper classification of land cover types, and require algorithms of pattern recognition. However, the precise performance of diverse ML algorithms during this process remains unclear for multispectral images. Here we show that the process of multi-step fitting in image analysis drives the predictive modelling of the landscape dynamics depending on the selection of the algorithm. We present the application of four ML algorithms using Geographic Resources Analysis Support System (GRASS) Geographic Information System (GIS): Gaussian Naive Bayes (GND), Decision Tree Classifier (DTC), Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) and Support Vector Machine (SVM). We found in supervised classification that SVM has the better capability of pattern recognition with enhanced accuracy and efficiency of image analysis. Time-series satellite Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS data spanning spring seasons of 2014–2024 was retrieved from the United States Geological Survey (USGS) platform. The aim is to improve the classification of the RS data processing through multi-step image classification. The methodology includes training, fitting and prediction. The effectiveness of ML algorithms was tested to evaluate their efficiency in classification of a time series of the satellite images using threshold  and multi-step fitting. The results demonstrated landscape dynamics in the coastal and desert areas of Eritrea. The results show that in 2014 the bare land or sparse vegetation class has the largest percentage (15,253 $km^2$ or 42\% of the total), followed by shrubs (8,024 $km^2$ or 21.7\%), herbaceous vegetation (5,268 $km^2$ or 14.8\%) and agricultural crops (1714 $km^2$ or 4.2\%). In 2024, the largest increase for herbaceous vegetation was detected in the area of Semenawi Keih Bahri (> 726.25 $km^2$), while the largest decline category of bare lands with 760 $km^2$. The increase in land cover types was recorded for the classes urban areas built, cultivated agricultural lands, and forests, while shrubs and herbaceous vegetation declined during recent decade.} 

\keywords{Artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, remote sensing, GIS, sustainable development; multistep classification; Africa; GRASS GIS; image analysis}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec1}

\subsection{Background}

Landscape dynamics and climate change have an intertwined nature, and affect the sustainability of the Earth's ecosystems and their inhabitants \cite{Conradi,atmos16040466,Ramachandra}. Given the essential role of landscapes for biodiversity, food security and social well-being, environmental monitoring of land cover changes is one of the most urgent challenges in Earth sciences, and is crucial for meeting the sustainable development goals \cite{Liuetal,Gao,geohazards6030058}. Landscapes in Eastern Africa vary in land cover types and vegetation coverage \cite{Lane,Lemenkova202206,Admas,Lemenkova202220,Jonas,Lemenkova202401,analytics2030040}. Factors that determine such variability are fundamental for land management policy and environmental monitoring. The most essential ones include climate and hydrological parameters which control water, temperature and precipitation, vital for life cycle.
 
Understanding reasons of environmental dynamics in context of land cover development plays a key role for predictive ecosystem modelling. In African countries, notable for highly heterogeneous landscape mosaic, such information can be used by environmental planners and climate analysts to model seasonal environmental rhythms \cite{Reiner2023}. Early detection of landscape dynamics needs a timely recognition of subtle land cover changes and involves data-driven monitoring. This is possible through information extraction from the Remote Sensing (RS) data processed using Geographic Information Systems (GIS), as reported in relevant studies \cite{w17182744,Gui,jimaging11080249,w17152317,Lemenkova202501}. Such methods enable quantifying land cover changes over short-term or long-term frames.

Nonlinear aspects of landscape dynamics and impact factors of land cover changes often remain hidden information. Extracting this information from satellite images is a challenge in eco-informatics which requires multi-step fitting and predictive analysis. Main issues are related to geo-information retrieval include RS data processing. Such techniques focus on image classification and interpretation of pixels for thematic mapping. Effective RS data analysis enables to detect landscape patterns \cite{asi6040061,Lemenkova20234964} or map geologic features \cite{DEFFO2024e26319,NWAILA202271,MONO2024135,ASGHEDE2025101722}. When applied to image analysis, GIS methods aims at finding the best solution for extracting geo-information through multi-step fitting. Hence, algorithms of image analysis aim at increase of precision and automation of the workflow. Such advancements are beneficial for analysis of landscape dynamics since they increase accuracy of mapping, forecasting and predictive modelling.

Nevertheless, there are still issues in image analysis related to workflow automation. These include, for instance, misclassified pixels, similar spectral reflectance values, ambiguous interpretation, and more. To solve this problems, Machine Learning (ML) presents effective supplementary advanced tools to image processing through algorithms of supervised ensemble learning \cite{plants14182841}. Such methods are especially effective for scale-fitted data analysis with growing area including the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) to automatically recognise objects on the images. The existing applications include both multispectral \cite{Lemenkova20253769,horticulturae11091073} and hyperspectral images \cite{rs17173126,horticulturae11091077} processed for environmental analysis. Other examples include mapping using Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) data for monitoring natural and man-made ecosystems, including land areas \cite{s25164991,rs17152733}, forests \cite{f16091381,rs17173011}, and coastal zones \cite{rs17162912}. In this study, we consider these approaches and present the case of ML algorithms for image analysis in GIS aimed at environmental monitoring of the spatially constrained region in East Africa.

\subsection{Motivation}

Univocal, consistent and reproducible approaches of cartographic data processing is an unresolved topic in ecology and environmental mapping. Progress towards accurate explanatory environmental modelling using combination of Machine
Learning (ML) techniques and RS data is limited in the coverage of similar GIS-based research for ecosystem modelling. With this regards, automation in GIS methods supports rapid and accurate data processing for spatially constrained regions. Specifically, Artificial Intelligence (AI)-driven cartography offers novel methods in geospatial tasks through training, evaluating and deploying ML models \cite{jmse12081279,Vahidnia02012019,Chenetal2023}. Over the course of recent decades, the development of programming algorithms and ML has challenged the GIS community to employ these analytical instruments for RS domain and cartographic data processing \cite{rs17122070,rs17162917}. 

Examples of ML include Deep Learning (DL), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Machine Learning (ML) techniques include robust algorithms of classification, such as RF, SVM, Naive Bayes, Gradient boosting, to mention a few. Application of such methods enhances the precision of object recognition on the image through advanced level of pixel analysis. Hence, AI significantly facilitates the workflow of image processing through process optimization: the increase of the speed and precision of data processing. As a result, the application of such methods improves the quality of image analysis.

Selected of the currently known ML methods in cartography should be briefly mentioned: Pyramid scene parsing network (PSPNet) \cite{ijgi12010014}, DeepLabV3 (PyTorch) \cite{rs17183145}, UNet \cite{rs14020358}, TensorFlow and Keras libraries of Python \cite{rs11232881}, Segment Anything Model (SAM) Meta-AI \cite{rs16091505}, DL algorithms \cite{w16081141}, or TensorBoard toolkit in ArcGIS Deep Learning Studio with Application Programming Interface (API) for Python by arcis.learn module. Such methods enable to perform image analysis workflow, including semantic segmentation, feature extraction, pattern recognition and supervised classification.

For environmental monitoring, the application of ML presents high perspectives since it presents automated framework to detection and robust attribution of land cover changes \cite{6069282,10170600,Lemenkova20201734,8526926}. As a result, rapidly developing advanced methods are now actively adopted in environmental analysis. To mention a few of them: multi-criteria analysis \cite{w17081212}, Monte Carlo simulating \cite{app15126575,w12071913,w16162302}, integer linear programming \cite{buildings14010213}, thresholding in image analysis \cite{AFJAL2019514}, combinatorial optimization of graph convolution network \cite{ijgi11050270}, adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference \cite{ijgi10060382,Pradhan2019}, solutions for optimization \cite{land12091786,Lemenkova202312,su16135660}, spatial interaction with predefined functional centres \cite{Kim03032016,urbansci5010007}, and many more examples of simulated modelling. Using such methods, it is possible to detect changes in land cover types on satellite scenes.

The study goal is to monitor the environmental changes in landscapes of Eritrea, East Africa. To achieve this goal, the general objective is to {quantify land cover changes detected on the satellite images. Specific objective is to evaluate the effectiveness of the ML methods of image processing through selected optimal algorithm in GRASS GIS \cite{grass_development_team_2025_14918754}. 
 
 \subsection{Research questions}
 
{Land cover change affects the domains of vegetation, plant diversity, habitat heterogenety, agriculture and environmental sustainability. Nevertheless, many questions remain unanswered. In this study we aim at answering the following research questions:}  
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Did landscape dynamics increase in eastern Eritrea in recent decade ?
	\item How did these changes affect landscape mosaics in the time span of 2014-2024 ?
	\item Did natural ecosystems gain compared to artificial surfaces and bare lands ?
\end{enumerate}
Answering these questions is crucial for understanding the applicability of landscape dynamics to other mountain areas of East Africa. To this end, we aim at resolving the technical tasks of RS data processing using ML:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Selecting optimal classification algorithm (LDA, SVM, DTC and GND);
	\item Properly exploiting the spectral-temporal information in the time series;
	\item Evaluating landscape variability over large areas with heterogeneous mosaic.
\end{itemize}

{In recent decades, Eritrea underwent a process of polarisation in the land use. On the one hand, lowlands and coastal areas experienced an expansion of bare or sparsely vegetated areas, and an intensification of shrubs or herbaceous vegetation to 43\%} \cite{Adem}, {often with the loss of the last remnants of cultivated lands and croplands due to the land abandonment. At the same time, the country includes precious biodiversity hotspots and fertile soil valuable for agriculture and food security, which is threatened by desertification} \cite{VALE202057,ANGULOPRECKLER2024e03339}. {Typical} landforms of Eritrea are related to the {mountainous geomorphology} which as formed by constant uplift at high rate and faulting during the Cenozoic era \cite{Bosworthetal2005}. Crustal deformations largely control the orientation and density of the main fluvial systems, affect the upstream and geomorphometric features (curvature and steepness) \cite{Abbate}. As a result{,} physiographic feature of Eritrea consists in its highly rugged relief {that reflects the tectonic uplifting in the past}: deeply incised river network and steep bedrock of a valley \cite{Billi2022b}. 

Large terranes are structurally and lithologically distinct and constitute the Neoproterozoic of Eritrea shaping its current geomorphology \cite{Markwick,Vanmaercke}. Close location to the tectonically active region of East African Rift with high magmatism associated with Cenozoic flood basalts in the Afar Triangle creates high seismicity \cite{Hammond}. {The country is located on} three major geological provinces:
 \begin{enumerate}
 	\item Western and northern regions cross the South Red Sea Shield with dominating highlands. The geometry of the Red Sea rift is shaped by the combination of the extensional stress field and lithospheric structures \cite{Issachar}. Tertiary faults activated shear zones in the Pan-African basement and caused active mantle processes in crustal basement \cite{Ebinger,Sembroni}.  
	 \item Eastern and south-eastern regions{ }are situated within the Amhara Plateau which continues central highland plateau of Ethiopia, and coastal regions of the Red Sea Basin. Active seismicity and volcanism in the past affected its modern relief \cite{HamielBaer}.
	 \item Southern part{ }continues the Afar Triple Junction with Danakil Depression. {It is }formed as a result of geologic-tectonic and climate factors. Here, the fork in the rift where three tectonic plates are diverging in the Horn of Africa results in high seismicity and active regional volcanism \cite{Illsley,Lemenkova202205}.  
 \end{enumerate}

{Such contrasting }geomorphic {landforms} in arid{ }desert climate {have} a strong influence on the {hydrogeological setting} and environmental patterns. These are expressed in the extent of the geologic lineaments, distribution of shield basalts and{ }hot springs. {The} areas of high groundwater potential are typical for basaltic layers located over the crystalline rocks. Such zones have flat topography, dense pattern of{ }structurally controlled{ }channels that enable water drainage in valleys and support vegetation growth. In contrast, Danakil Depression consists of sedimentary rocks (sandstone and limestone) overlain by basalts originating from lava flows during past volcanism.

\subsection{Data}

The{ }data were collected from the United States Geological Survey (USGS) {EarthExplorer repository} as a time series {of the multispectral satellite images Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS} on {the month of March in }years 2014, 2018, 2022 and 2024{, Figure} \ref{fig05}. {The quality of data makes it possible to exploit the synergy of the multi-temporal time series.} The {data} contained seven multispectral Landsat bands for each image (Aerosol, Blue, Green, Red, NIR, SWIR-1, SWIR-2){.} 

{Colour} composites of the images {are based on the} following multispectral bands{: }3-4-5; 1-5-6; 1-4-6; 5-4-3. Band designations for Landsat -8 OLI/TIRS sensor used for this visualization: 1- Coastal aerosol; 3 – Green; 4 – Red; 5 – Near Infrared – NIR; 6 – Shortwave Infrared (SWIR)-1. Common characteristics of the are the following: Datum and ellipsoid are WGS84, Product Map Projection UTM Zone 37. Sensor Identifier is OLI\_TIRS, Landsat Satellite 8, Collection Category T1, Number 2. The images were taken at day time at nadir, with quality taken at scale 9 with Ground Control Points (GCP) Version 5, Station Identifier LGN, WRS path 169, row 49. Metadata and key technical characteristics of the images{ }are summarised in the Appendix \ref{AppA}. {The choice of these data is explained by the reputable history of Landsat, free availability and quality. }Geospatial data are important source {of information }for analysis of landscape dynamics {and environmental mapping}\cite{5383529,Lemenkova202212,5653756,Lemenkova202125}. {RS }data enables to model biodiversity changes{ using time series analysis} \cite{Feigl,Pangtey2025,Singh2025}. 

{Various studies on land cover mapping are carried out with satellite data, e.g., SPOT-4, WorldView, Landsat. Examples of the Landsat include the cases of environmental monitoring of Africa} \cite{BHAGA2023101037,FEYISA201423}. {This study employs the Landsat-8-9 OLI/TIRS images, whose characteristics are similar to those of Sentinel-2. Despite the difference in resolution (30 m for multi-spectral bands of Landsat, 10 m for visible and Near Infra-Red (NIR) bands for Sentinel, both sensors share common important geometric features of performing acquisitions at constant angles. Thus, the scenes are observed with the same solar and acquisition angles, which is important geometric specifics. \subsection{Machine learning}

{To enrich the functionality of GIS, the ML solutions were used through algorithms of Gaussian Naive Bayes (GND), Decision Tree Classifier (DTC), Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) and Support Vector Machine (SVM). }The ML classifiers were used to detect {land} cover types {on the} images.{ }Unlike the existing methods of image classification, ML considers the differences among the spectral reflectance of pixels when modelling topology of patches. {Besides, } ML {analyses} texture and spectral features to measure the similarity of {neighbour} patches {while} generating random decision trees.{ }All{ }models were trained {through} randomly selected points from the test image.{ }The technical approach employs $n\_estimators$ parameter in every ML model, included in the syntax of the code. Here, the number of estimators parameter is derived from Scikit-Learn library of Python and affects only tree-based estimators{. }In this context, it specifies the number of separate decision trees trained on various subsets of observations{.} The data were trained using the function $training\_map$ in the 'r.learn.train' module{.} The fitted { }estimator was applied to rasters using 'r.learn.predict'{.}  The {ML algorithms were} evaluated for performance with the categories checked using the 'r.category' module{ to evaluate the overfitting through several cycles of reassessment.}

\begin{itemize}
\item {The} pixels were evaluated and assigned using the r.learn.predict {which predicts} the {assignment} of pixels to land cover classes.
\item {The} training pixels were employed to perform a classification on the recent images using the {r.learn.train}.
\item Raster categories were automatically applied to the{ }output and assessed using ‘r.category’.
\end{itemize}

The same procedure was completed for all the maps and visualised for GNB approach of ML. The key approach of the GNB as a ML classification technique consists in an assumption that supposes that each land cover class follows a normal distribution. It presumes that each parameter of vegetation reflectance has an independent capacity of predicting the output variable. This is advantageous for homogeneous classes. However, for landscapes of Eritrea, the input data are heterogeneous and selected classes (xeric vegetation in semi-desert areas and mangroves along the coasts of the Red Sea, mosaic grasslands and shrubland) do not contain such normal distribution and have regional distribution patterns.

\subsubsection{Algorithm: Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)}

The major approach of the LDA algorithm is that it solves the multi-class classification problem in highly diversified patterns of landscape with repetitive mosaics. It separates multiple classes which have diverse several features by reducing the data dimensionality. {The mathematic foundation for the case of LDA with more than two classes is presented in the Equation} \ref{eq01}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq01}
	\Sigma _{b}={\frac {1}{C}}\sum _{i=1}^{C}(\mu _{i}-\mu )(\mu _{i}-\mu )^{\mathrm {T}}
\end{equation}

{Here, the assumption is that every C class has the same covariance $\Sigma$ and a mean of the categories $\mu _{i}$. The sample covariance of the means of each land cover class is then used to define the scatter between class variability. Such principle approach increases the precision of the separability between the classes.} The main feature of the LDA consists in the optimization of the ML models for image processing through partition of classes that represent land cover types \cite{rs17050854}. 

 \subsubsection{Algorithm: Support Vector Machine (SVM)}
 
{The Support Vector Machine (SVM) algorithm has been effectively applied in remote sensing. The goal of SVM is to find the best borders between the classes by fitting the separating values in high dimensions. The SVM is a group of linked learning algorithms, and can be applied both for classification and regression tasks. Originally developed by} \cite{VapnikChervonenkis}, {the SVM becomes a robust non-parametric classifier widely used in many cases on image analysis} \cite{earth5030024,rs16122250,rs16122219}. {The process of SVM is based on training the data with linearly separable classes which determines the quality of its performance. Its mathematical implementation consists is described in the Equation} \ref{eq02}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq02}
	\left[{\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{i=1}^{n}\max \left(0,1-y_{i}(\mathbf {w} ^{\mathsf {T}}\mathbf {x} _{i}-b)\right)\right]+\lambda \|\mathbf {w} \|^{2}
\end{equation}
 
{where $(\mathbf {x} _{1},y_{1}),\ldots ,(\mathbf {x} _{n},y_{n})$ signify the training image dataset of $n$ pixels that form raster matrix of cells, ${w}$  is the normal vector perpendicular to the linear hyperplane, which indicates the discriminating direction of the plane, and a scalar $b$, which indicates the discriminating hyperplane's offset from the origin, necessary for separation of the land cover classes The majority of the natural land cover classes obey the soft-margin classifier, hence, a small value for $\lambda $ results in the hard-margin classifier for satellite images with medium resolution, such as Landsat. Such feature of the SVM is suitable for image classification in heterogeneous landscapes with nonlinear borders between land cover classes.}

\subsubsection{Algorithm: Decision Tree Classification (DTC)}}
  
{Decision Tree Classification (DTC) algorithm is among the most widely used categorization models due to their interpretability and applications in remote sensing} \cite{Miao,Buthelezi,Meyer}. {Based on the recursive partitioning of the dataset, it becomes the efficient and scalable learning algorithm for image classification. The DTC method iteratively divides the dataset as the entity of pixels on the raster image according to their characteristics -- Digital Numbers (DN), representing spectral reflectance. This iterative partition minimizes the impurity of the classified dataset, using biodiversity index of Gini impurity which indicates feature importance score} \cite{rs16101659}. {Branches produced in this procedure to represent attribute values of the land cover categories according to the spectral reflectance. The key mathematical foundation of this method is the following. The probability of miscategorizing the pixel on the raster image is defined in Equation }\ref{eq03}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq03}
	\sum_{k\neq{i}} p_{k} = 1 - p_{i}
\end{equation}

{where $p_i$ is the probability of choosing a pixel with label $i$ on the raster image $ i\in \{1,2,...,J\}$ which should be categorised into the correct class. Here, the set of pixels comprise the image with $J$ classes and relative frequencies $p_{i}$ for each pixel indicating cell on the raster scene. For each label of land cover class, the pairwise products of these probabilities are added up to determine the Gini impurity as in Equation }\ref{eq04}:

 \begin{equation}\label{eq04}
	I_{G}(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{J}\Biggl(p_i\sum_{k\neq{i}} p_k\Biggr) = \sum_{i=1}^{J} p_i(1-p_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{J} (p_i - p_{i}^2) = \sum_{i=1}^{J} p_i - \sum_{i=1}^{J} p_{i}^2 = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^{J} p_{i}^2
\end{equation}

{The algorithm of the DTC includes four key components :} 
\begin{enumerate}
	\item {Root Nodes show the starting points of the tree before dividing the image into classes} 
	\item {Decision Nodes representing the points of the branches which go from these nodes}
	\item {Branches that connect nodes and show paths  of the results of the tests}
	\item {Leaf Nodes signifying the final nodes of the decision tree as class labels}
\end{enumerate}
{The depth of the trees is controlled by the parameters of minimum number of leaf node observations and maximal number of decision splits. In this case we chosen four for the depth of the DTC. The decision nodes are selected according to the attributes, i.e., class features of the pixels showing spectral reflectance values of the land cover types.}

\subsubsection{Algorithm: Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB)}
   
{Theoretically, the Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB) uses a conditional probability model through assigned probabilities $p(C_{k}\mid x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n})$ for each of the $K$ possible results or classes $C$ on the classified satellite image as $C_{k}$ where pixels representing the scene of the images are described as vector $ \mathbf {x} =(x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n})$ in Equation} \ref{eq05}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq05}
	p(C_k \mid x) = \frac{p(C_{k})p(x \mid C_{k}))}{p(x)}
\end{equation}

{The ML algorithm GNB is based on Gaussian distribution and employs probabilistic techniques. Its mathematical foundation is the Bayes theorem which presumes that features are conditionally independent. Hence, each variable of the pixel of the image representing spectral reflectance of the Earth's objects follows a normal distribution. The algorithm determines the likelihood that an unknown pixel falls into the given category of land cover class by computing the mean and variance of the spectral reflectances in each category of land cover class} \cite{rs16122168,Lietal2022,Madhu}. {In this way, the algorithm partitions the  mosaic of land cover classes into spatial pattern of categories with distinct values. Since the majority of the land cover features on the image have roughly normal distributions, the GNB model can be applied to image processing with acceptable justification.}

The results of experiments demonstrate the following outcome of the ML methods. The computational results indicate that the SVM algorithm of the is dominant in comparison with other methods. Compared to the clustering algorithm of unsupervised classification, SVM algorithm achieved the best performance in classification of the satellite images. The parameter values of the SVM showed that most regions were identified correctly except for the occasional segments in the landscapes of central Eritrea, Figure \ref{fig09}. SVM outperformed the other approaches due to the following main reasons indicating its effectiveness: 

\begin{itemize}
\item First, the algorithm is highly effective for high dimensionality data such as RS characterised by the heterogeneous mosaics of landscapes, as in case of Eritrea. 
\item Second, due to kernel function, SVM is suitable to solve complex problems of pattern recognition in constrained landscapes with diversified environmental patches. 
\item Third, compared to the other ML and {Artificial Neural Network (ANN)} approaches, SVM is less prone to overfitting which results in accurately classified scenes of the multispectral imagery. 
\end{itemize}

The drawbacks of the DTC for image processing include the instability to changes and noise in the dataset (even low cloudiness might affect the output). Besides, the algorithm is based on complex calculations and is computationally expensive with need for computer memory and resources which is demanding for large datasets and limited computer memory. Finally, the classified categories are unbalanced as the algorithm is non-continuous. The LDA{ }is sensitive to outliers which means that selected patches of landscapes can be ignored. Besides, the LDA might miss local geometric information in highly rugged landscape patches with irregular shapes of the polygons which limits its robustness and efficiency in such cases and complex mosaics of landscape patches. \section{Conclusion}\label{sec13}

{Despite the existing applications of ML in image analysis, there is still} lack of a practical framework and hands-on environmental applications {for African studies. The environmental dynamics }needs to be better exploited in view of the{ vulnerability of the landscapes to cumulative effects from climate, human activities and geologic setting}. Hence, this study demonstrated the {contribution to the environmental analysis of East African region showing the }benefits of ML {in image analysis}. The examples of the supervised training in ML approaches {were applied to image analysis} with a case of the {land cover} mapping of Eritrea{from 2014 to 2024}. The research evaluated ML algorithms of image classification for improved workflow of image processing and tested their applicability in cartographic works.{. }Maps generated from the processed {Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS} satellite images demonstrated {landscape dynamics in 10-year period}.  Landscape dynamics includes a complexity of both human-induced and climate-related processes. Landscape dynamics is triggered by a consequence of the expansion of forest area and the habitat homogenization {which can be detected using approach of eco-environmental informatics} \cite{Yue2025}. 

Time series analysis of spaceborne data enables to compare current and previous state of the landscapes and highlight landscape dynamics using cartographic data analysis. Such visualization can be used for crop monitoring \cite{OUATTARA2025104783,LIEPA2024101230}, assessment of landscape sustainability \cite{6573358,Demichelis} and prognosis of {the environmental} development \cite{Subramani,Sprigg2025}. In this work, the supervised {classification} of { images} were {compared} to map landscape dynamics using various ML {algorithms}. These include the important characteristics of RS data processing, such as speed and accuracy, randomness of nodes, analytical determination of the output weights, and dependence distribution of pixels for each algorithm. Though each{ }algorithm was developed to serve different objectives of{ }RS data processing, their technical implementation and practical purposes present valuable approaches to{ }image analysis. 

The{ }applications {of ML algorithms }can be effectively used for environmental monitoring and mapping \cite{Ezugwu}. {Thus, ML-based RS data processing has seen a significant increase in ecological research on sustainable development and strategic land planning. Natural hazards including droughts, desertification and deforestation, can be identified using RS data to support appropriate decisions. Locations of threats can be identified using geospatial cartographic data processing which presents great importance geoinformatics. ML has been accepted as a robust novel approach to finding solutions to satellite image processing. }When using ML approach, the automation of geospatial data processing increases according to the choice of diverse ML instruments \cite{Basu}. 

Moreover, the feasibility of the ML algorithms enables to use it as an innovative approach that may be used in practice for diverse types of satellite images. Here we demonstrated that the outcome of the framework in the GRASS GIS based image processing using ML is the output of the series of the classified satellite images covering central Italy processed using decision criteria analysis of ML algorithms. The particular case of this work presented an example of the ML modules to process the multispectral Landsat images for environmental mapping of Eritrea. Several algorithms of ML were tested and compared for visualization of landscapes using image partition into land cover classes automatically. To enhance its application in similar future studies, the ML classifiers technique can be applies as effective tools of GRASS GIS to processing Earth observation data for operative environmental monitoring.
